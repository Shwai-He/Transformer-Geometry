\section{Intervention Details}
\label{app:method}

\paragraph{Attention-diagonal editing.}
Equation~\ref{eq:general-diagonal-constraint} defines the requested change in
the parallel component. Let $\mathbf m_t$ be the self message,
$\mathbf r_t$ the reference, and $\mathcal B_{tt}$ its linear map into the
edited space. One minimum-Frobenius-norm realization of
$\Delta\mathbf y_{tt}=\Delta\mathcal B_{tt}\mathbf m_t$ is
\(\Delta\mathcal{B}_{tt}^{\star} = \bigl(s^{(\parallel)}-1\bigr)
\frac{\mathbf{y}_t^\top\mathbf{r}_t}{\|\mathbf{m}_t\|^2\|\mathbf{r}_t\|^2}
\mathbf{r}_t\mathbf{m}_t^\top\).
For a structured operator family, the same construction uses the Frobenius
projection of \(\mathbf{r}_t\mathbf{m}_t^\top\) onto the admissible operator
subspace. Table~\ref{tab:effective-diagonal-derivation} gives the resulting scalar forms.
For the exclude-self variant in our primary value-space results,
Eq.~\ref{eq:exclude-self-scaling} applies projection to
\(\sum_{s<t}A_{ts}\mathbf{v}_s\), restoring direct \(A_{tt}\mathbf{v}_t\).

\paragraph{Applied strength of the two edits.}
Section~\ref{sec:diagonal-validation} and Table~\ref{tab:effective-diagonal-derivation}
show that exclude-self value-space editing preserves the self contribution
$A_{tt}\mathbf{v}_t$, inducing bounded non-positive shifts along the attention diagonal,
whereas residual-space removal forces volatile signed swings ($-1.00$ to $+0.76$).
However, diagonal stability does not imply a gentler intervention.
Table~\ref{tab:applied-no-para-strength} audits both Full No-Para interventions at the
post-$W_O$ output, comparing edited $\widetilde{\mathbf{y}}_t$ against baseline $\mathbf{y}_t$.
The exclude-self value-space edit induces a substantially larger perturbation
on every metric: $49.10\%$ norm change and $25.89\%$ energy removal, versus
$25.15\%$ and $9.32\%$ for residual-space removal.
This divergence reveals a geometric asymmetry: residual-space removal
prunes only the 1D projection along incoming $\hz{}{t}$, which is a smaller fraction of branch
norm yet fatally disrupts inter-layer residual propagation.
Conversely, value-space editing zeros parallel components across the non-self
aggregate $\sum_{s<t} A_{ts}\mathbf{v}_s$, discarding significant contextual energy
while preserving the primary diagonal identity channel.
Preserving capabilities despite perturbing outputs nearly twice as heavily
shows that stability depends on structural alignment rather than isotropic magnitude.

\paragraph{Head-level localization.}
\label{app:head-localization}
Our primary interventions suppress parallel components across all attention heads jointly.
A key mechanistic question is whether sensitivity originates from isolated outlier heads or reflects distributed capacity across the multi-head ensemble, and whether single-head edits already produce disruption.
To test this, we compare isolated single-head parallel removal against joint all-head removal across four Qwen3 scales (Figure~\ref{fig:per-head-causal-sensitivity}).
This reveals two core properties. First, \textit{single-head resilience}: editing any single head produces negligible loss change (blue envelopes remain near zero), showing mutual redundancy where neighboring heads compensate for isolated subspace removal. Second, \textit{super-linear joint degradation}: simultaneously suppressing parallel components across all heads (orange curve) degrades loss by orders of magnitude beyond the single-head envelope; multi-head edits eliminate the layer's collective representational channel.
Finally, layer~0 exhibits the sharpest joint disruption across scales, showing early token routing relies critically on shared multi-head parallel coordinates.

\section{MLP-Internal Geometry}
\label{app:mlp-internal-carrier}

Residual-space editing observes only final MLP output $y = W_{\mathrm{down}}h \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
Inside the MLP, however, the post-gating carrier $h = \phi(W_{\mathrm{gate}}x)\odot W_{\mathrm{up}}x \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mlp}}}$ has a wider dimension than the residual state $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d_{\mathrm{mlp}} > d$), so direct projection between $h$ and $x$ is undefined.
To align dimensions while preserving the unedited forward pass, we partition the $d_{\mathrm{mlp}}$ coordinates into $G = d_{\mathrm{mlp}}/d$ disjoint blocks $I_g = \{(g-1)d+1,\dots,gd\}$ of width $d$.
By block matrix multiplication, $y = W_{\mathrm{down}}h = \sum\nolimits_{g=1}^G y_g$, where each carrier slice $h_g = h[I_g] \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and block contribution $y_g = W_{\mathrm{down}}[:,I_g]h_g \in \mathbb{R}^d$ shares dimension $d$ with $x$.

With directional scaling $\mathcal{P}(u; r) = s_\parallel \frac{u \cdot r}{\|r\|^2}r + s_\perp \left(u - \frac{u \cdot r}{\|r\|^2}r\right)$,
the two internal editing sites in Table~\ref{tab:mlp-internal-carrier} are:
\begin{align}
  \widetilde{y}_{\mathrm{carrier}} &= \sum\nolimits_g W_{\mathrm{down}}[:,I_g]\,\mathcal{P}(h_g; x), \label{eq:mlp-site1} \\
  \widetilde{y}_{\mathrm{contrib}} &= \sum\nolimits_g \mathcal{P}(y_g; h_g). \label{eq:mlp-site2}
\end{align}
Site~1 (\emph{grouped $h$ rel.\ to $x$}) edits intermediate carrier $h_g$ before down projection;
Site~2 (\emph{grouped $y_g$ rel.\ to $h_g$}) edits projected contribution $y_g$ before group summation.
These controls isolate directional asymmetry within intermediate representations from down-projection effects.
On Qwen3-0.6B, full parallel removal shifts fixed C4 loss by at most 0.0301, whereas perpendicular removal increases
it by $>13$ points (Table~\ref{tab:mlp-internal-carrier}).

\paragraph{Downstream task validation.}
While fixed C4 loss (Table~\ref{tab:mlp-internal-carrier}) confirms that internal parallel removal preserves perplexity, cross-entropy averages over easy tokens and can mask structured reasoning degradation.
To test functional stability, Table~\ref{tab:mlp-internal-downstream} audits Full No-Para ($s_{\parallel}=0$) and Full No-Perp ($s_{\perp}=0$) across Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B on Core-MC (9 tasks), MMLU (5-shot), GSM8K, TruthfulQA, and DROP/CoQA.
Full No-Para preserves downstream reasoning across scales: Core-MC ($50.98$ vs.\ $50.94$ on 0.6B; $55.28$ vs.\ $55.19$ on 1.7B) and MMLU ($52.66$ vs.\ $52.40$; $60.39$ vs.\ $60.21$) match baselines.
Conversely, Full No-Perp collapses catastrophically: MMLU plummets by $25.5$/$35.8$ points to near-chance guessing, and Core-MC falls $14$--$18$ points.
Thus, parallel components carry dispensable scaling, while perpendicular updates encode essential factual and reasoning knowledge.

\section{Pretraining Setup and Evaluation}
\label{app:training-configs}

\paragraph{Architecture and pretraining setup.}
Pretraining trajectories (Figure~\ref{fig:pretraining}) train standard Transformer models from scratch on OpenWebText~\citep{Gokaslan2019OpenWeb} (296M/436M/528M) and FineWeb100BT~\citep{penedo2024the} (1.4B/2.7B) with all-layer value-space parallel removal.
Dense configurations use standard GQA: 1.4B ($d=2048$, 24 layers, 16/4 heads); 2.7B ($d=2560$, 32 layers, 32/8 heads).
Models train on $\approx$104.9B tokens over 200K steps (batch 256, length 2,048; lr $4\times10^{-4}$/$3\times10^{-4}$, 2K warmup, cosine decay, AdamW $\beta=(0.9,0.95)$, decay 0.1, clip 1.0; 8$\times$H100 in BF16).

\paragraph{Downstream evaluation results.}
Table~\ref{tab:appendix-pretraining-downstream} resolves summary downstream averages from Table~\ref{tab:pretraining-summary} (\S\ref{sec:pretraining}) into benchmark scores across ARC-E, BoolQ, HSwag, OBQA, PIQA, and WinoGr. Value-space removal consistently outperforms the baseline ($+0.7$ on 1.4B, $+1.5$ on 2.7B), confirming robust inductive bias across tasks.

\paragraph{Gated parallel-scaling diagnostic.}
To test whether parallel scaling requires learned modulation, we compare fixed removal ($s_{\parallel}=0$) against tokenwise learned gating ($s_{\parallel} = \sigma(w^\top x)$) on 1.4B pretraining (Figure~\ref{fig:appendix-1p4-trainable-scale}). Fixed removal maintains lowest loss throughout, showing parallel suppression acts as an inductive bias.

\paragraph{Alignment with cosine diagnostics.}
Our directional decomposition mathematically aligns with cosine diagnostics for layer dropping~\citep{He2026UncoveringTR}.
For residual state $x$ and combined block update $\Delta = \alpha x + \Delta_\perp$ ($\|\Delta_\perp\|=\beta\|x\|$), cosine similarity is $\cos(x,x+\Delta) = (1+\alpha)/\sqrt{(1+\alpha)^2+\beta^2}$.
As baseline updates are small ($\|\Delta\|/\|x\| \ll 1$; Figure~\ref{fig:baseline-update-over-hidden}), $\alpha,\beta \ll 1$, yielding $1-\cos(x,x+\Delta) \approx 0.5(\|\Delta_\perp\|/\|x+\Delta_\parallel\|)^2$.
Angular diagnostics thus evaluate perpendicular steering normalized by parallel magnitude.
