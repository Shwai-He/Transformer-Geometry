\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Transformer layers evolve representations through learned additive
updates~\citep{vaswani2017attention,elhage2021mathematical} on top of residual
paths~\citep{he2016deep}. Geometrically, each update has two roles relative to
the current representation: a parallel component that changes magnitude while
preserving direction, and a perpendicular component that redirects it. This
view frames representation evolution as a functional geometry problem:
decompose learned updates by their directional role, then test their behavioral
sensitivity through targeted interventions.

Across pretrained models, we find substantial parallel components in the
learned updates themselves. They are distinct from the exact carryover supplied
by the residual identity path: attention and MLP branches also learn updates
aligned with the representations they modify. Their presence alone, however,
does not establish whether they are redundant, behaviorally important, or
sensitive to the space in which alignment is defined.

We study this question in two spaces. In \textit{residual space}, we decompose
the attention or MLP update relative to the hidden state to which it is added.
In \textit{value space}, we decompose the pre-output-projection attention
aggregate relative to the current token's own value. Because the full aggregate
combines the direct self message with values routed from other positions, we
also introduce an exclude-self variant that preserves the direct self message
and decomposes only the non-self aggregate. Component scaling in these spaces
lets us distinguish the general geometry of an attention aggregate from the
specific directional role of cross-token aggregation.

The interventions reveal a pronounced directional and space-dependent
asymmetry. Perpendicular scaling is consistently disruptive, whereas the effect
of parallel scaling depends on where the component is defined and what the edit
preserves. Residual-space parallel removal substantially changes model
behavior, and full-aggregate value-space removal is also nontrivial. In
contrast, preserving the direct self message makes value-space parallel scaling
markedly more robust across perplexity, downstream tasks, and long-context
evaluation. This robustness is not explained by a uniformly smaller
perturbation: at a common post-projection site, the exclude-self value-space
edit is larger than its more fragile residual-space counterpart. Robustness
is therefore not a generic property of being parallel; it depends jointly on
the representation space, reference direction, and preserved computation.
Similar-looking parallel-removal operations therefore solve different
geometric objectives, explaining why their behavioral effects diverge across
representation spaces.

Finally, we ask whether the same geometry is informative beyond inference-time
editing. For compression, we decompose the error between baseline and
compressed-model updates, complementing prior work on post-training compression
and layer-dropping analyses of transformer
redundancy~\citep{Frantar2022GPTQAP,Lin2023AWQAW,Sun2023ASA,men2024shortgpt,He2026UncoveringTR}.
Across quantization and pruning, perpendicular-error profiles separate
compression methods more clearly than parallel-error profiles.
Motivated by the inference-time asymmetry, we separately test full-aggregate
parallel suppression during from-scratch pretraining. Extensive experiments
demonstrate that the value-space variant yields lower validation-loss
trajectories across model scales. Both residual- and value-space variants
improve average downstream scores, with the value-space variant producing the
larger gains. \begingroup\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}The contributions are:
