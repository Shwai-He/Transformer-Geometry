\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Inference-time interventions.}
For pretrained-model evaluations, edits operate only during the forward pass
and leave model weights unchanged. Value-space results use the
self-preserving scaling in Eq.~\ref{eq:exclude-self-scaling} unless explicitly
labeled otherwise; each experiment reports its edited layers and scales.
Appendices~\ref{app:method}--\ref{app:evaluation-details} give implementation details.

\paragraph{Pretraining setup.}
For pretraining, we train three GPT-style configurations from scratch on
standard next-token prediction, with baseline and full-aggregate
parallel-removal variants.
We additionally train 1.4B and 2.7B models for downstream evaluation
(Table~\ref{tab:pretraining-summary}). Model configurations and the released settings are in Appendix~\ref{app:training-configs}.

\section{Directional Robustness of Updates}
\label{sec:editing-diagnostics}

\subsection{Component Scaling}
\label{sec:zeroshot}

\paragraph{Component-scaling results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:residual-component-editing} sweeps \(\spara\) over $[-1,3]$
and \(\sperp\) over $[0,2]$ at three sites. On the parallel grid, exclude-self
value-space scaling is stable within the unit interval: full removal changes
PPL by only 0.46 and the largest absolute change is 1.32, whereas removing
either residual-space parallel component increases PPL by about 21--22.
The stable interval has a clear sign boundary: under reversal, the value-space
delta reaches 188.77 at scale $-1$. Perpendicular scaling produces larger
changes: at scale $2$, the deltas are 212.68 in value space, 14,757.67 for the
residual attention branch, and 52.62 for the residual MLP branch. The sweep
therefore shows both
directional and representation-space asymmetries. Residual-space edits act on
a complete learned branch, whereas exclude-self scaling isolates the non-self
parallel component while preserving the remaining aggregate. The same
directional asymmetry is already present inside the MLP: decomposing the
post-gating carrier rather than the branch output leaves full parallel removal
near baseline while perpendicular removal remains disruptive
(Appendix~\ref{app:mlp-internal-carrier}).

Appendix~\ref{app:method} formalizes this distinction. \textit{The scaling
sweep uses component editing as a functional probe. We next turn the probe into
a sharper removal test of whether value-space parallel removal preserves
performance on standard downstream and long-context evaluations.}

\subsection{Value-Space vs.\ Residual-Space Editing}
\label{sec:value-vs-residual-exp}

\paragraph{Robustness on general tasks.}
Table~\ref{tab:general-zeroshot} reports the full-removal comparison on the
seven non-generative tasks. Under the matched residual/diagonal protocol,
Attn Para-Rem. changes the average by $-2.46/-2.94$ points on the
1.7B/30B-A3B models, whereas Diag. Rem. changes it by $-22.44/-32.97$ points.
Under the value-space protocol, exclude-self removal stays within 1.51 points
of the 1.7B baseline and 0.12 points of the 30B-A3B baseline, whereas editing
the self contribution as well reduces the average by 8.2--10.0 points.
Exclude-self removal is markedly more robust because it preserves the self edge
and leaves the remaining attention normalization unchanged; hard diagonal
removal is far more disruptive because it removes the self edge and
renormalizes the remaining attention. Thus robustness depends on both the
representation space and preservation of the direct self message.

Having established the behavioral gap between value- and residual-space edits,
we next inspect how their parallel-removal objectives appear as diagonal
coefficient changes.

The residual-space coefficients span $-1.00$ to $0.76$, whereas the selected
head's exclude-self value-space coefficients are
non-positive, with the first-token coefficient exactly zero because there is
no non-self context. The difference reflects their reference directions and
the post-$W_O$ mixing used by residual-space editing. Coefficient magnitude is
not intervention magnitude: a Qwen3-0.6B diagnostic measures both edits at the
same post-$W_O$ attention output and finds perturbation
norms of 25.15\% of the original branch norm for the residual-space edit,
versus 49.10\% for exclude-self value-space editing
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:applied-no-para-strength}). Residual-space fragility
therefore does not follow from a larger applied perturbation in this
comparison.

The all-layer averages also conceal substantial head and depth heterogeneity.
Appendix~\ref{app:head-localization} reports isolated-head sensitivities and
joint all-head controls across four Qwen3 scales.

\textit{The removal experiments show that model behavior is especially sensitive
to direction-changing components of activation updates.} We next ask how this
picture appears when the model itself is modified. Compression changes the
parameters, so its effect is not an explicit activation edit; instead, it
induces an error between the baseline and compressed-model updates. We
decompose this error to test whether its perpendicular component distinguishes
compression settings more clearly than its parallel component.

At each layer, we define the compression error as
\(e=\Delta_{\mathrm{comp}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{base}}\) and decompose it relative
to the baseline-update direction:
\(e_{\parallel}=\mathrm{proj}_{\Delta_{\mathrm{base}}}e\) and
\(e_{\perp}=e-e_{\parallel}\). Figure~\ref{fig:local-flip-attn} reports the
component norms normalized by \(\|\Delta_{\mathrm{base}}\|\) and averaged over
fixed prompts.

Perpendicular-error curves separate the four settings more clearly, while
parallel-error curves interleave. Thus the direction-changing error provides
the more discriminative component-level diagnostic in this comparison.
Appendix~\ref{app:compression} provides branch-wise views, the derivation, and
the supporting baseline-update magnitude plot.

\section{Training-Time Allocation}
\label{sec:pretraining}

The inference-time results show that the effect of suppressing parallel
components depends strongly on the representation space in which the
intervention is applied. We therefore ask whether applying the same
intervention throughout training changes how optimization allocates learning
across the retained, direction-changing updates. We train from scratch with
residual- or value-space parallel removal active during both optimization and
evaluation, avoiding a train--inference mismatch. We compare the two
interventions through their training trajectories and downstream scores;
Appendix~\ref{app:training-configs} gives the configurations and gated-scaling
control.

Figure~\ref{fig:pretraining} shows that suppressing parallel attention updates
changes the optimization trajectory. The separation from baseline appears
early and remains visible across all three configurations through the final
checkpoint, rather than emerging only as an endpoint difference.

Table~\ref{tab:pretraining-summary} reports downstream evaluation for the
larger configurations. Attn Para-Rem. improves the average over baseline, and
V-Para Rem. gives the strongest average at both scales, with gains of $0.7$
and $1.5$ points. Together with the loss curves, these results show that
parallel suppression affects both the training trajectory and the resulting
checkpoint, with a larger downstream change for the value-space variant.
