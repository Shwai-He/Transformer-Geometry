\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Compression diagnostics.}
Perpendicular error is more fragile than direction-preserving error in our
controlled edits. However, its ranking is nearly identical to total-error
ranking and is matched by a norm-controlled isotropic null. We therefore use
it as a component-resolved diagnostic, not as predictive information
independent of total magnitude.

\paragraph{Practical screening workflow.}
For an edit or compression rule: (1) specify its location and reference;
(2) run matched directional ablations; (3) compare orientation with total
error and a matched null; and (4) screen geometrically before downstream
validation. Prevalence alone is insufficient because sensitivity varies with
depth and joint head effects are non-additive.

\paragraph{Training-time allocation.}
The training runs test whether suppressing the comparatively robust
value-parallel route reallocates optimization pressure. Full-scale single runs
improve average downstream score, whereas an early matched three-seed study
improves validation loss but not the six-task average. This supports changed
optimization and operation-dependent co-adaptation, not a replicated
downstream gain or convergence claim.
