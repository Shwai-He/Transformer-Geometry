\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we formalized representation evolution in Transformers through directional orthogonal decomposition, separating learned updates into parallel magnitude modulation ($\Delta_\parallel$) and perpendicular semantic steering ($\Delta_\perp$). Across pretrained language models, our interventions uncovered a universal directional asymmetry: representations exhibit an expansive, resilient tolerance basin for parallel scaling, but acute, fragile sensitivity to perpendicular perturbations. We demonstrated that this geometric framework provides practical utility across the model lifecycle: resolving discrepancies in post-training compression by identifying perpendicular error as the true causal driver of degradation, and guiding from-scratch pretraining to lower validation-loss trajectories and improve downstream generalization. These results establish directional geometry as a principled foundation for deciphering internal representations, diagnosing model compression, and guiding future architecture design.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our experiments cover decoder-only language models across multiple scales,
language tasks, inference-time interventions, compression settings, and
from-scratch training configurations. Although the same geometric picture is
consistent across the evaluated settings, its behavior under other
architectures, modalities, and post-training regimes remains to be established.
Future work should test whether these directional patterns persist across such
settings and connect layer-level geometry more directly to downstream behavior.
We use the proposed edits primarily as analysis tools; turning them into
practical training or inference methods requires further validation.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}

Directional decomposition provides a concrete tool for auditing representation
change and screening compression or interventions before downstream evaluation.
This screening requires no retraining and is designed to complement
task-specific testing and human review. Responsible deployment should evaluate
prompt- and context-specific behavior as well as bias, privacy, security, and
high-stakes use. Access controls and downstream monitoring are appropriate when
deploying editing capabilities.
